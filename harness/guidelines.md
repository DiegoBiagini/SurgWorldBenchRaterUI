# Surgical Video Generation Evaluation Guidelines

## Objective

This study evaluates how realistic and instruction-compliant synthetically generated surgical videos are.

Each video is a **6-second generated clip**. The model receives:

- an initial frame
- a text instruction

Several videos may be shown for the same initial frame and instruction, generated by different models in random order.

Your task is to score each video on three criteria:

1. **Instruction following**
2. **Surgical tool consistency**
3. **Anatomy plausibility**

Each criterion is scored from **1 to 5**.

# Evaluation Criteria

## 1. Instruction Following

This measures whether the generated video successfully performs the requested action.

Scores range from **1 to 5**.

A score of **3 or higher** means the instruction was successfully completed.
A score below **3** means the instruction was not successfully completed.

| Score | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **1** | The instruction is completely ignored. Nothing relevant happens, or the opposite/wrong action happens. Example: the tool is instructed to move right, but moves left instead. |
| **2** | There is an attempt, but the goal is not reached. Example: the tool moves slightly in the correct direction but not far enough to satisfy the instruction. |
| **3** | The goal is reached, but the motion is unnatural, partial, or contains clear intermediate mistakes. Example: the tool moves erratically but eventually ends in the correct target region. |
| **4** | The instruction is followed correctly, but the motion still feels somewhat unnatural or imperfect. |
| **5** | The instruction is followed perfectly, with clear intent, smooth motion, and correct execution. |

### Summary

- **5**: Perfect execution.
- **4**: Correct, but slightly unnatural.
- **3**: Goal reached, but with noticeable issues.
- **2**: Attempted, but failed.
- **1**: Completely wrong or ignored.

---

## 2. Surgical Tool Consistency

This measures whether the surgical tool keeps a plausible, stable appearance throughout the video.

Scores range from **1 to 5**.

| Score | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **1** | The tool heavily warps, disappears, reappears, or morphs into unnatural shapes. |
| **2** | The tool mostly keeps a recognizable shape, but that shape is highly unnatural or unrealistic. |
| **3** | The tool looks plausible at a surface level, but joints, tips, handles, or moving parts behave strangely. Some parts may not look like real surgical instruments. |
| **4** | The tool looks almost correct, with only small visual artifacts during movement or interaction. |
| **5** | The tool is indistinguishable from a real surgical tool. This is expected to be extremely rare. |

### Difficulty weighting

Be stricter for simple tasks.

For example:

- A simple hook movement should have high visual consistency.
- A complex task involving a needle driver or needle transfer is harder, so small artifacts may be more acceptable.

### Static-video case

If the video is almost static:

- Tool consistency should usually receive around **3**.
- The tool may look okay, but it is not realistically behaving or operating.

---

## 3. Anatomy Plausibility

This measures whether the anatomy looks and behaves realistically.

Consider:

- tissue shape
- tissue deformation
- tool-tissue interaction
- liquid behavior
- pulsation or motion
- anatomical realism
- physical plausibility

Scores range from **1 to 5**.

| Score | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **1** | Anatomy behaves completely unnaturally. There is severe distortion, physically impossible motion, or structures that do not belong in a surgical scene at all. |
| **2** | Anatomy behaves strangely but does not fully collapse visually. Some surfaces or structures look wrong, but the scene remains somewhat physically coherent. |
| **3** | Anatomy is plausible at a surface level, but there are several issues in fine details such as deformation, tool contact, fluids, or tissue response. |
| **4** | Anatomy looks very good, with only small artifacts or minor issues in detailed interactions. |
| **5** | Anatomy is indistinguishable from reality. This is expected to be extremely rare. |

### Difficulty weighting

Be stricter when the anatomy is not being manipulated much.

If little is happening to the tissue, the anatomy should remain very stable and realistic.
If the tissue is being actively manipulated, some artifacts may be more understandable.

### Static-video case

If the video is almost static:

- Anatomy plausibility should usually receive **2 or 3**.
- It may not distort, but it also does not behave like real living tissue in a realistic surgical video.

# Task Types

The generated videos represent one of the following task types.

---

## A. Control Tasks

In control tasks, a single surgical tool is selected as the tool that should move.
This is usually the **leftmost tool** in the initial frame.

The instruction specifies where the selected tool should end up by the end of the video.

The possible target locations are:

- `top-left`
- `left`
- `bottom-left`
- `top-right`
- `right`
- `bottom-right`
- `forward`
- `backward`

The task is considered successful if a relevant enough section of the selected tool reaches the requested final target location (e.g. most of the tip, a big chunk of the body).

A visual guide for these target locations is shown in the image below:

[Control task target locations](control_task_locations.png)

### Image-plane targets

For 2D image-plane movement, judge the final tool position relative to the camera view.

The target regions are defined as follows:

| Target | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `top-left` | The tool ends in the upper half of the image and within the left third of the image width. |
| `bottom-left` | The tool ends in the lower half of the image and within the left third of the image width. |
| `top-right` | The tool ends in the upper half of the image and within the right third of the image width. |
| `bottom-right` | The tool ends in the lower half of the image and within the right third of the image width. |
| `left` | The tool ends within the left third of the image width, regardless of vertical position. |
| `right` | The tool ends within the right third of the image width, regardless of vertical position. |

Note that `left` and `right` are broader targets than the corner targets.
For example, a tool ending in the upper-left region may satisfy `top-left`, but for a `left` instruction, the important requirement is simply that the final position is on the left side of the image.

### Depth targets

For depth movement, judge whether the tool moves relative to the camera:

| Target | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `forward` | The tool ends farther away from the camera than it started. |
| `backward` | The tool ends closer to the camera than it started. |

For `forward` and `backward`, the tool may also move slightly in the 2D image plane.
However, the key criterion is whether its final depth is correct relative to the camera.

---

## B. Needle Passing Tasks

There are two needle drivers.

One needle driver initially holds a needle.

Possible instructions:

1. Pass the needle to the other needle driver.
2. Pass the needle to the other needle driver, then pass it back to the first needle driver.

The task succeeds if the needle is transferred as requested.

### Scoring note

If the needle transfer technically succeeds but the grasping, handoff, or needle behavior is strange, penalize instruction following.

For example:

- If the needle appears to teleport or is passed with unrealistic grasping, a score of **3** is appropriate.

---

## C. Anatomy Manipulation Tasks

A tool and a tissue region are specified.

The instruction gives a movement direction:

- `up`
- `down`
- `left`
- `right`

The tool should drag or move the tissue in that direction relative to the camera.

The task succeeds if the tissue clearly moves in the instructed direction.

### Scoring note

Use judgment for the minimum acceptable displacement.

- Large, clear movement in the correct direction: higher score (4 or 5).
- Visible movement: moderate score (3).
- Barely noticeable movement: low score (2).
- Wrong direction or no tissue movement: very low score (1).
